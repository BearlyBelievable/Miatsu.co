This page documents features specific to Miatsuco's fork of Zulip. They are
not part of upstream Zulip, and won't appear in Zulip's own documentation.

## Configure whether previews of uploaded images and videos are shown

<AdminOnly />

You can configure whether uploaded images and videos are shown as inline
thumbnail previews in the message feed, or as plain links to the file. This
does not affect uploaded audio files, which are always shown as a playable
inline preview.

See also: [Image, video and website
previews](/help/image-video-and-website-previews) for the related, upstream
Zulip settings controlling previews of linked (non-uploaded) images, videos,
and websites.

<ZulipNote>
  Thumbnails are generated for uploaded images and videos regardless of this
  setting. However, Zulip renders each message's content once and caches it;
  changing this setting does not retroactively update how already-sent
  messages are displayed. It affects new messages going forward, and any
  existing message that is later edited.
</ZulipNote>

<FlattenedSteps>
  <NavigationSteps target="settings/organization-settings" />

  1. Under **Message feed settings**, toggle **Show thumbnail previews of
     uploaded images and videos**.

  <SaveChanges />
</FlattenedSteps>

## Configure whether you see image, video, and website preview thumbnails

You can turn off inline thumbnail previews in your own view for uploaded
images and videos, linked images and videos, and website previews, showing a
compact link you can click to load the preview on demand instead. This can
help save bandwidth, or reduce how much visual space media takes up in the
message feed. This is a personal preference; it does not change what other
people in the conversation see.

<ZulipNote>
  This does not affect uploaded audio files, which are always shown as a
  playable inline preview. Previews of uploaded images and videos
  specifically are also subject to your organization's own [upload preview
  setting](#configure-whether-previews-of-uploaded-images-and-videos-are-shown),
  if it has been turned off there.
</ZulipNote>

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Desktop/Web">
    <FlattenedSteps>
      <NavigationSteps target="settings/preferences" />

      1. Under **Information**, toggle **Show image, video, and website
         preview thumbnails**.
    </FlattenedSteps>
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Configure who can send direct messages without an authorizer present

<AdminOnly />

By default, Zulip lets you require that certain users (for example,
moderators or admins) be included in every direct message conversation, by
setting **Who can authorize a direct message conversation**. This fork adds a
companion setting, **Who can direct message others in this setting without an
authorizer present**, which lets you name a group whose members may exchange
direct messages among themselves without one of those authorizing users
present.

This is useful when you want direct messages to generally require an
authorizer, but still allow a trusted group (for instance, everyone in a
given team) to message each other freely.

<ZulipNote>
  An individual can choose to be stricter than this allowance for their own
  direct messages; see [Restrict who can direct message
  you](#restrict-who-can-direct-message-you).
</ZulipNote>

<FlattenedSteps>
  <NavigationSteps target="settings/organization-permissions" />

  1. Under **Direct message permissions**, set **Who can direct message
     others in this setting without an authorizer present**.

  <SaveChanges />
</FlattenedSteps>

## Restrict who can direct message you

You can choose to only take part in direct message conversations where every
other participant is able to authorize direct messages (a member of your
organization's **Who can authorize a direct message conversation** group,
such as moderators or admins). When this is enabled, a direct message that
includes anyone who cannot authorize it will not be delivered, even if an
authorizing user is also included.

This is a personal setting, and it is stricter than any organization-level
allowance: it applies even to users who could otherwise message you without
an authorizer present. It also applies to direct messages you send, since a
direct message is a two-way conversation.

<ZulipNote>
  Because this depends on your organization's **Who can authorize a direct
  message conversation** group, the setting shows who can currently authorize
  your direct messages once you enable it.
</ZulipNote>

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Desktop/Web">
    <FlattenedSteps>
      <NavigationSteps target="settings/account-and-privacy" />

      1. Under **Privacy**, toggle **Restrict direct messages to only those
         who can authorize them**.
    </FlattenedSteps>
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>
